## A first game, brought to an ending

Clone Number Eight was my first game, made in Unreal Engine over roughly two to three months. Its first releases were around February 2020. It was inspired by the short films I was making at the time, with an abandoned building as the setting for a first-person parkour route.

<Video index={1} group open />

## A route to the roof and back

The opening goal is to climb to the roof, collect a star, and make the descent. Completing it opens a basement where the player can choose what to do next: a block-building sandbox or a set of parkour challenges.

The sandbox and challenges use separate scenes. There were roughly three to four challenge levels, and completing all of them led to the final ending. I originally wanted a shooter, but narrowed the first project to parkour when implementing weapons and enemies proved too much at that stage.

<Gallery indices={[1, 2]} group open />

## Planning what the player would see

Hand-built level design became the strongest part of the project for me. I drew plans on paper before assembling the scenes, thinking through the route, what the player would see, dead ends, and alternative ways through.

The mushroom challenge is one of my favourites. I spent time making the levels feel distinct through their palettes, arrangement of objects, and obstacles, rather than relying on one repeated environment.

<Video index={2} group open />

## The last challenge in the cave

The final cave is the other level I remember most fondly. Together with the mushroom level, it represents the attention I gave to planning and assembling spaces in this first project. The surviving walkthrough records the finished game from beginning to end.

<Video index={3} group open />

## Connecting the levels with Blueprint

I built the game logic while learning from tutorials. My Blueprint work included character movement, triggers for changing levels, tracking completion, and opening the basement with the next game modes. The project gave those separate lessons a concrete purpose: making a game that could actually be completed.

<Video index={4} group open />

## My level design, with ready-made assets

I assembled the levels myself using ready-made models and assets. The work shown here is the layout, staging, routes, and game logic, rather than original modelling of the environment props. Music and sounds were also ready-made downloads; their original sources are no longer known. The selected archival videos are presented without sound.

<Image index={6} group open />

## A completed first step

No playable build has survived. The development recording and full walkthrough preserve the result: an early game with working modes, several challenges, and an ending. I keep it here as the beginning of my work in games and a project where I put particular care into designing levels by hand.
