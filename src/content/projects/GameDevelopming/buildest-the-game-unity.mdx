## Returning to a procedural world

The Unity version was a new attempt to develop the idea of Buildest The Game. I wanted a terrain with height variation and a stronger survival focus. The surviving archive begins in December 2022 and records several stages and later returns; I finally stopped development on 5 May 2025.

The last build remained a technical prototype: moving, jumping, creating and saving worlds, and changing some settings worked. Resource gathering, crafting, and building never became a working gameplay loop. The forest and character images on this page document intermediate stages.

## Shaping a mesh with noise

I generated my own mesh and deformed its vertices using FastNoiseLite. Different combinations of noise shaped experiments with hills, plains, mountains, rivers, and biomes. The archived terrain screenshots show stages of that same generator.

Tree placement also depended on noise: processing a terrain vertex triggered a check for a structure at that location. I experimented with caves, but never found a satisfactory way to turn the surface mesh into enclosed underground spaces.

## Managing chunks in one place

A single manager tracked the chunks around the player. It converted the player’s world position into the coordinates needed for chunk placement, checked lists of occupied positions, and determined where new chunks were required.

I wrote the C# code myself; AI served as a consultant and suggested solutions. Readability and extensibility were priorities because I expected to add much more content. The project helped me practise organising generation and the surrounding game code.

## A changing visual direction

The visuals moved from more detailed forest experiments towards simpler, stylised trees. Alongside the generator, I made a wooden character, its mechanical rig, and first-person animations. These were development stages, not a complete set of features preserved in the final download.

The game version with the doll and its movement animations was lost. The surviving models, animation exports, and screenshots are presented in their own portfolio pages.

## Interface work around an unfinished game

Creating and saving worlds worked, along with settings such as mouse sensitivity and draw distance. Other menu options remained disconnected from gameplay. I also developed coordinated animations for buttons and screens, combining Unity animation clips, Animator, and code.

I created the project’s visual work and music myself, using FastNoiseLite as the terrain-noise library. Some model references were AI-generated, as noted on the modelling page. My track Adventure played in the menu.

## When optimisation stopped the game moving forward

Large numbers of objects repeatedly became a performance problem. I kept trying to reduce the rendering cost, while the playable part of the project barely progressed. In the later build available on itch.io, I removed extra animation and the doll character and focused on performance and extensibility.

Stopping the project made the imbalance clear: I had concentrated on generation and optimisation without forming a complete gameplay loop. The useful result was experience with procedural generation, object-oriented code, and internal organisation that later helped me build IsLands.
