## TrashToCash · Item systems

An unfinished recycling-game prototype from 2026. I wrote item factories, storage logic, and foundations for saving. The working interactions covered dismantling objects into materials, collecting them in containers, and selling them. The complete save-and-load system was not finished.

I focused on readable, structured code that could support more content. The footage from the September development post shows a primitive-based test scene; the textured low-poly assets are presented separately on the modelling page.

## Driving prototype · Chunks ahead of the car

In August 2020, I made an Unreal Engine free-driving prototype. The vehicle controller was ready-made; I connected it to the controls and interface, then assembled and scripted environment chunks from assets. The player controlled acceleration, without points or rewards.

Triggers started the creation of chunks ahead of the car, extending the route during the drive. Chunks left behind were removed when they became sufficiently distant from the player. This generation system was my main contribution to the prototype.

## 3D Shooter · Animation and photo textures

An unreleased Unreal Engine shooter built with ready-made weapon models and my own Blueprint logic. I animated firing, reloading, weapon switching, camera movement, and drawing and holding a phone with a music player. In an unreleased version, enemies could pursue the player through NavMesh, shoot, take damage, and die.

I also experimented with materials made from my photographs of an abandoned building. I corrected the colours, cleaned up details, and adjusted the images to the required resolution before applying them to simple box-shaped geometry.

## BitPixel · A procedural pixel-art stick

The most distinctive experiment in BitPixel was a stick drawn entirely by a shader. Resolution, seed, and colouring could be changed to produce different pixel-art versions of the same kind of object. I wanted the generated shapes to retain a hand-drawn appearance.

The prototype also had an endless 2D world, character movement, and early attempts at generating trees. It was an early exercise in more structured object-oriented code. Drawing a custom character and crafting with collected pixels remained ideas.

## Butterfly · Volume through 2D light

A December 2021 Unity prototype about a butterfly. The implemented behaviour was flying and landing when idle. I drew the textures and normal maps by hand and used URP 2D lighting to give flat sprites a sense of volume through the butterfly’s light and the lights in the scene.

The broader idea was a vulnerable butterfly travelling through dangerous dungeons, inspired by games such as Soul Knight. Procedural rooms did not reach a working implementation; the surviving scene records the visual and movement experiments.

## 2.5D block world · Mining and inventory

An Unreal Engine experiment inspired by Terraria: a noise-generated block world, several terrain layers, block destruction, and particles. I drew the textures myself. The prototype explored these systems on a much smaller scale than my later IsLands project.

The inventory supported picking up blocks, storing and stacking them, and moving them between cells. Placing blocks back into the world from the inventory was not implemented.

## Dungeons / Magic Sticks · Mobile movement

A mobile dungeon-game prototype with my own drawings. I implemented joystick movement, a test room, a weapon-switch button, character animations, and character stats. The visual effects in the recordings are particles around the character or staff, not working shots.

The intended game revolved around collecting staffs with different properties. Shooting, damage effects, and dungeon generation were not implemented. The surviving work is a study of character presentation, controls, and switching staffs.

<Gallery indices={[14, 15]} group open />

## Sweather · A small survival prototype

A less procedural survival-game experiment with world generation, character movement, and character animations. Unlike BitPixel’s custom procedural graphics, this prototype used external textures.

The top item panel was only an inventory interface. Picking up, storing, or using items through it had not been implemented.

<Image index={16} group open />

## Tic-Tac-Toe · A pencil-style interface

An unfinished tic-tac-toe project with a pencil-drawn visual style. The computer opponent initially chose moves almost randomly; I later added precomputed responses to specific board situations as an interim solution. This was a rule-based opponent, not a learning system.

Facebook multiplayer was explored but not completed. The surviving images document the menu design and interface work in Photoshop, rather than a demonstration of online play.
